## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** RL Training — world-model RL (DreamerV3 via process-isolated JAX subprocess). NOT an LLM/agent/RAG/chatbot system; the LLM framework matrix (CrewAI/LangGraph/LlamaIndex/OpenAI Agents SDK/Claude Agent SDK/etc.) is eliminated by problem-class mismatch.

**Description:**
A reinforcement-learning training integration that replaces 5 stub functions (`_build_agent` / `_train_loop` / `_evaluate` / `_save_checkpoint` / `_load_checkpoint`) in `src/surg_rl/dreamer/subprocess.py` with real DreamerV3 training implementations against the PyPI `dreamerv3~=1.5.0` object-based API (`dreamerv3.Agent` + `embodied.run.train` + `embodied.Checkpoint`). The user is an RL researcher training a learned policy on a simulated surgical task. "Good" = the subprocess trains a real DreamerV3 agent end-to-end, checkpoints persist and resume per task/obs-type, JAX never leaks into the parent process, the JSON-over-stdio pipe stays clean, and the inverted Phase 30 sentinel guards against stub regression.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **API drift** — targeting the `danijar/dreamerv3` GitHub-main factory API instead of the PyPI 1.5.0 object API the pin installs (resolved by D-04; the stubs MUST use `Agent(obs_space, act_space, step, config)`, not `make_agent`/`elements.*`).
2. **Checkpoint resume breaks across subprocess restart** — `embodied.Checkpoint` attribute-registration pattern (`cp.agent=agent; cp.replay=replay; cp.step=step; cp.load_or_save()`) misapplied so training does not actually resume (DMV3-08).
3. **JAX leaks into the parent process** — any `import jax` / `import dreamerv3` at module top in `subprocess.py` breaks SC#5 and the macOS/parent import path; imports must stay inside child-process function bodies.
4. **JSON pipe corruption** — the embodied logger writing to stdout would corrupt the JSON-over-stdio protocol; logger must redirect to stderr (D-locked).
5. **Silent stub regression** — `_build_agent` returns `None` again and nobody notices because the sentinel was deleted instead of inverted (DMV3-09 guards this).

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Surgical robotics research tooling / robot-assisted surgery RL research engineering. NOT a clinical device — `PROJECT.md` explicitly scopes out "FDA certification / medical-grade safety validation — Research and simulation tool, not clinical device". The downstream consumer of trained policies (clinical deployment on a dVRK/STAR-class robot) is a separate regulatory pathway; this phase ships the training pipeline that produces a checkpointed policy, not the policy-in-the-OR.

**User Population:** Two populations, both internal to a surgical-robotics RL research lab:
1. **Surgical-robotics / RL researchers** — run `surg-rl dreamer-train` to train a DreamerV3 world-model policy on a simulated surgical task (suturing, knot-tying, needle insertion, grasping, cutting, dissection). They care whether training actually ran end-to-end, whether the checkpoint they resume from tomorrow is the one they stopped at today, and whether the metrics stream is trustworthy enough to decide "keep training vs. change hyperparams".
2. **ML-research-infrastructure engineers** — own reproducibility, checkpoint/resume correctness, CI GPU spend, and the JAX/PyTorch coexistence on shared GPU hosts. They care whether JAX stays inside the subprocess, whether the GPU job fires only on merge-to-main (not per-PR), and whether a stub regression is structurally impossible to merge.

**Stakes Level:** High. Not Critical (no patient in the loop; research tool, not clinical device). But High because: (a) GPU-runner spend is metered and a misconfigured per-PR GPU job burns budget silently; (b) a silently-broken stub (`_build_agent returns None`) produces a "training run" that yields no real policy — a researcher can waste days believing they have a checkpointed agent when they have nothing (this exact regression is why the Phase 30 sentinel exists); (c) checkpoint-resume failure throws away multi-hour GPU runs; (d) non-reproducible training undermines the publication-grade benchmarking the platform is built for.

**Output Consequence:** Downstream, a researcher loads `models/dreamerv3/{task}_{obs_type}/checkpoint.ckpt`, resumes training, runs `agent.policy` rollouts for evaluation, and reports results in a benchmark. When the output is correct: a usable, resumable, checkpointed policy + a trustworthy per-batch METRICS stream. When the output is silently wrong: (1) a "checkpoint" that doesn't resume (step counter restarts at 0 → wasted GPU-hours + non-reproducible numbers in a paper), (2) a "trained agent" that is actually `None` (no policy at all — the regression the sentinel guards), (3) NaN/Inf metrics treated as "training is hard" instead of "training is broken", (4) JAX preallocating GPU memory in the parent process so PyTorch OOMs on the same GPU. The damage is measured in wasted GPU-hours, retracted/benchmark-invalidating numbers, and researcher trust in the pipeline.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Dimension: Checkpoint resume continuity
Good: Restart the subprocess with the same `{task}_{obs_type}` dir → the step counter picks up at ~500 (not 0), the metrics log shows steps 500→1000, and the agent/replay/step are restored from `checkpoint.ckpt`. Verified by a restart-then-continue test on the GPU host.
Bad: Restart re-trains from step 0 (silent loss of the prior run's compute); OR a `Too many leaves for PyTreeDef` PyTree mismatch is caught-and-continued silently instead of surfacing as ERROR; OR task A's checkpoint dir is reused for task B (cross-config collision).
Stakes: Critical
Source: DreamerV3 README troubleshooting ("Too many leaves for PyTreeDef" on incompatible checkpoint / reused logdir); practitioner RLOps — atomic checkpoint writes + full RNG-state restore (darthmanwe/Training_Distributed_Systems); D-09 attribute-registration pattern.

Dimension: Training-loop structural integrity (finite, non-explosive)
Good: Over a 1000-step smoke run, every METRICS value is finite (no NaN/Inf), and the loss does not explode beyond an order of magnitude over its initial value. The `checkpoint.ckpt` file exists at the end. This is asserted as STRUCTURAL ONLY — not the v0.4.0 spike's `MSE<0.01` convergence threshold.
Bad: NaN/Inf in any metric; loss grows unbounded across batches; no checkpoint file written; OR a convergence threshold is silently re-introduced (over-claims policy quality from a 1000-step smoke).
Stakes: High
Source: DreamerV3 paper §app stability (KL spikes → unimix; symlog/symexp to prevent divergence from large targets; LaProp + AGC instead of Adam); DMV3-10 smoke-vs-convergence split.

Dimension: JAX process isolation (parent import stays clean)
Good: `import surg_rl.dreamer` in the parent (the GUI/CLI/test-collection path) never imports `jax` or `dreamerv3`; `pytest tests/ -m "not integration"` collects in seconds, not 30+; the parent process allocates zero GPU memory; all `import jax`/`import dreamerv3`/`import embodied` live inside the 5 stub function bodies (call-time, child-only).
Bad: A top-level `import jax` in `subprocess.py` loads JAX in the parent → preallocates 75% of GPU memory (default `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION`) → PyTorch OOMs on the same GPU; OR setting `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION` AFTER `import jax` (no effect — JAX reads it at import time).
Stakes: Critical
Source: JAX GPU memory allocation docs (`XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION` default 75%, must be set before `import jax`); JAX discussion #16177; PROJECT.md Key Architecture Decisions (process isolation via JAX subprocess, `MEM_FRACTION=0.4`).

Dimension: Stub-regression guard (sentinel inversion, not deletion)
Good: The Phase 30 E2E test is INVERTED in place — its `pytest.raises(RuntimeError, match="Agent not configured")` assertions become positive real-agent-completion assertions (agent ran, `checkpoint.ckpt` exists, no RuntimeError). A NEW source-inspection test (`test_dreamerv3_regression_guard.py`) fails closed if `_build_agent` can `return None` (AST `Return(None)` check or string match). The regression guard runs in the CPU matrix on every PR — no GPU needed.
Bad: The sentinel is DELETED instead of flipped (no regression signal); OR the regression guard is GPU-gated (so PR-time gets no signal); OR `_build_agent` returns `None` on a config edge case the AST check misses.
Stakes: High
Source: v0.6.0 research locked decisions (sentinel flip not delete); D-10; Phase 30 sentinel design (the stub-reality revision history the flip inverts). This regression already happened once — the sentinel exists because `_build_agent` returned `None` and shipped.

Dimension: GPU-spend discipline (CI firing model)
Good: The `dreamer-gpu` job fires on merge-to-main + release tags + manual `workflow_dispatch` ONLY — not per-PR. PR-time signal comes from the CPU-only regression guard + import-leak guard. `total_steps=1000`, `eval_every=500`, ~3-5 min/run. If GitHub GPU runners are not enabled on the account, this is documented as an ops enablement step in the PR — NOT silently left 100%-skipped.
Bad: GPU job fires per-PR (metered spend burn); OR the job is 100%-skipped in the milestone audit because the runner wasn't enabled and nobody documented it; OR convergence-threshold assertions are added back (turning a 5-min smoke into a multi-hour convergence job).
Stakes: Medium
Source: D-01/D-02/D-03; practitioner RLOps (AWS SageMaker RL — eval-first lifecycle, proxy metrics before full benchmark); metered GitHub GPU runner economics.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Stub-agent silent regression (already happened once).** `_build_agent` returns `None`; the subprocess protocol still emits CONFIG_ACK/METRICS-shaped messages; training "runs" but produces no real policy. Without an inverted sentinel + a source-inspection guard, this ships unnoticed. This is the regression the Phase 30 sentinel was built to detect — the failure mode is not hypothetical, it is the historical one.
2. **Checkpoint/config mismatch on resume (`Too many leaves for PyTreeDef`).** Reusing a `models/dreamerv3/{task}_{obs_type}/` dir after changing the agent config (obs_type, action dim, hyperparams) makes `embodied.Checkpoint.load()` fail with a PyTree-structure mismatch. In this system it also manifests as cross-task contamination if the `{task}_{obs_type}` scoping is mis-derived from the CONFIG message — task A resumes task B's weights and the researcher doesn't notice until eval. Must surface as ERROR, not swallow.
3. **JAX import-time GPU preallocation leak.** `import jax` (or any transitive import) at module top in `subprocess.py` runs in the PARENT process, preallocating 75% of GPU memory before `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION=0.4` can be set. Setting the env var after import is a no-op. Result: PyTorch OOMs on the same GPU; macOS dev boxes take 30+ seconds to collect tests. The fix is structural (imports inside function bodies) and must be guarded by an import-leak test, not reviewed into existence.
4. **Surgical-RL-specific policy degeneracy masked by "finite loss".** The surgical-RL literature (LapGym, SurRoL, SurgicAI) documents that policies can maximize shaped reward without solving the task (reward hacking in LigatingLoop), or fail to explore at all under sparse rewards + tiny surgical instruments (pure online RL → near-zero success). A 1000-step structural smoke that only asserts "loss is finite" CANNOT distinguish a learning policy from a degenerate one — which is exactly why DMV3-10 excludes convergence thresholds for THIS phase and why the eval-planner must not over-claim policy quality from the smoke. The smoke answers "did training run", NOT "is the policy good".

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** DreamerV3 (PyPI `dreamerv3~=1.5.0` object-based API) + the `embodied` runtime — a JAX world-model RL algorithm library. This is an RL training integration, not an LLM application.

**Version:** `dreamerv3~=1.5.0`, `jax~=0.4.20`, `optax>=0.1.7` (pins in `pyproject.toml` `[dreamer]` extra — UNCHANGED per D-04).

**Rationale:**
The entire LLM framework decision matrix (`ai-frameworks.md`) is eliminated by classification: this phase integrates a reinforcement-learning training algorithm, not an LLM/agent/RAG/chatbot system, so every LLM framework is ruled out by problem-class mismatch rather than by scoring. Decision D-04 in `40-CONTEXT.md` locks the target to the PyPI 1.5.0 object-based API — `dreamerv3.Agent(obs_space, act_space, step, config)` + `embodied.run.train` + `embodied.Checkpoint` — and resolves the STATE.md API-drift blocker (the factory API named in the blocker describes `danijar/dreamerv3` main, not what `pip install '.[dreamer]'` installs). The supported integration surfaces for this version are the manual `embodied.Driver` + `agent.train()` batch loop (D-07) and the `embodied.Checkpoint` attribute-registration resume pattern (D-09). The pin, the `GymToEmbodiedWrapper`, the `DreamerSubprocess` + `_JsonStdout` protocol, and the `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION=0.4` isolation are all reused unchanged.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| `embodied.run.train` high-level entry point | Documented fallback only (D-07). Its internal checkpoint+logging cadence would double-checkpoint and conflict with the subprocess protocol's own `CHECKPOINT`/`METRICS` message types. NOT the primary path. |
| `danijar/dreamerv3` current-repo factory API (`make_agent`/`make_replay`/`make_stream`/`make_logger` + `elements.*`) | Describes GitHub main, NOT what `pip install '.[dreamer]'` installs (D-04). Would introduce an `elements` dependency and miss the installed object API. Explicitly rejected — resolves the STATE.md API-drift blocker. |
| `embodied.wrappers.FromGym` | Pins `gym==0.19.0`, which conflicts with the project's `gymnasium>=0.29.0` (D-05). Reuse the existing `GymToEmbodiedWrapper` instead. |
| LLM agent frameworks (CrewAI / LangGraph / LlamaIndex / OpenAI Agents SDK / Claude Agent SDK / AutoGen / Google ADK / Haystack) | Wrong problem class — these are LLM/agent/RAG/chatbot orchestration frameworks; this phase trains a learned policy from simulation. No LLM is in the training loop. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial. Locked to the `dreamerv3~=1.5.0` PyPI object API by D-04 — the pin is frozen and the object API is the target, not the evolving GitHub-main factory API. This is a conscious trade-off: it bounds maintenance risk (the upstream API is drifting) at the cost of staying on 1.5.0 until a deliberate pin-bump phase. The `embodied` runtime ships inside the `dreamerv3` distribution. No external model-provider lock-in (the policy is trained from simulation, not a hosted LLM).

### Common Pitfalls
1. **API drift — PyPI 1.5.0 vs `danijar/dreamerv3` main.** The `~=1.5.0` pin installs the Feb-2023 PyPI object API (`Agent` 4-arg, `embodied.*`); the GitHub main branch is factory-based (`make_agent`/`make_replay`/`make_stream`/`make_logger`, `Agent` 3-arg, `elements.*`). Why: the blocker in STATE.md names the factory API from current-repo/DeepWiki research, not what `pip install '.[dreamer]'` ships. Warning signs: `ImportError: cannot import name 'make_agent'`, `TypeError: Agent.__init__() takes 3 positional arguments but 4 were given`, `ModuleNotFoundError: elements`. Mitigation: target the PyPI 1.5.0 object API; VERIFY every `embodied.*` symbol against the installed package before relying on it. **Open discrepancy:** the `b65cf81a` GitHub branch (1.5.0-era) shows `embodied.run.train(make_agent, make_replay, make_env, make_stream, make_logger, args)` (factory) and `elements.Checkpoint`, while 40-RESEARCH.md's tarball inspection found `embodied.run.train(agent, env, replay, logger, args)` (object) and `embodied.Checkpoint`. D-07 makes the manual Driver loop the primary path and `embodied.run.train` only the fallback, so this does not block the primary implementation — but the planner MUST run `python -c "import embodied; print(dir(embodied))"` in the child to settle `embodied.Checkpoint` vs `elements.Checkpoint` and `embodied.Config` vs `elements.Config` before writing `_build_agent`.
2. **gym vs gymnasium version conflict.** dreamerv3 1.5.0's `embodied.envs.FromGym` imports `gym==0.19.0`; the project uses `gymnasium>=0.29.0`. Why: gymnasium is a maintained fork with a different import path; installing both causes pip resolver conflicts and namespace errors. Mitigation: use the project's `GymToEmbodiedWrapper` (D-05), which already implements the embodied.Env protocol against Gymnasium. Never import `from_gym` from dreamerv3.
3. **JAX leaks into the parent process.** `subprocess.py` is imported by the parent (for `DreamerSubprocess`); a top-level `import jax` / `import dreamerv3` loads JAX in the parent, allocating GPU memory and violating SC#5. Why: the stubs live in a module that has a parent-imported class. Mitigation: ALL `import jax` / `import dreamerv3` / `import embodied` stay INSIDE the 5 stub function bodies (call-time, child-only); the `dreamer/__init__.py` `LazyImport` guard stays unchanged; the new `test_dreamer_subprocess.py` import-leak guard pins this. Warning signs: `pytest tests/ -m "not integration"` takes 30+ seconds to collect (JAX import overhead); parent shows GPU memory allocation.
4. **`agent.save(path)` instead of `embodied.Checkpoint`.** Upstream persists via `Checkpoint` wrapping agent+replay+step with `load_or_save()`; `Checkpoint.__setattr__` raises `ValueError` if the assigned object lacks both `save()` and `load()`. Calling `agent.save()` directly returns a numpy dict but does NOT write to disk — `Checkpoint._save()` is what writes. Why: the stub-era signature `_save_checkpoint(agent, path)` invites a direct `agent.save(path)` call, which silently does nothing on disk. Mitigation: `_save_checkpoint` / `_load_checkpoint` delegate to `cp.save()` / `cp.load_or_save()` on the bundle's `Checkpoint`, never to agent methods directly.
5. **Embodied logger corrupts the JSON pipe.** `_JsonStdout` replaces `sys.stdout` with a `pipe.send(payload)` call; the embodied `Logger`/`TerminalOutput` defaults to stdout (and may bypass `sys.stdout` via `sys.__stdout__` or direct fd writes), emitting non-JSON `loss=0.5` lines that make the parent's `json.loads` raise `JSONDecodeError`. Why: the subprocess's stdout IS the protocol pipe — any non-JSON write corrupts it. Mitigation (D-locked): configure the embodied logger to write to `sys.stderr` (which is `os.fdopen(2)`, a real fd) OR suppress `TerminalOutput` entirely and rely on JSONL file output + the METRICS messages shipped via the pipe. VERIFY the exact redirection mechanism against `embodied/core/logger.py` in the installed package.
6. **Checkpoint path collision across task/obs-type.** Why: the stubs receive a `path` arg, not a `{task}_{obs_type}` key; a hardcoded path makes task A resume task B's weights. Mitigation: the checkpoint dir MUST be `models/dreamerv3/{task}_{obs_type}/checkpoint.ckpt`, derived from the CONFIG message and passed through; `_find_latest_checkpoint` (`training.py:191-197`) globs `*.ckpt` (D-09), retiring the stub-era `checkpoint_*.pt` / `final.pt` glob with NO `.pt` compat shim.
7. **Incompatible-checkpoint `Too many leaves for PyTreeDef` on resume.** Reusing an old logdir with a changed config (different obs_type, action dim, or agent hyperparams) makes `Checkpoint.load()` fail with a PyTree mismatch. Why: the checkpoint tree structure encodes the agent's parameter structure. Mitigation: the `{task}_{obs_type}` dir scoping (Pitfall 6) prevents most cross-config collisions; if the config changes, bump the checkpoint dir or delete the stale one. Do NOT catch-and-continue this error — surface it as an ERROR message so the user sees the mismatch.
8. **100%-skipped CI audit failure.** If the `dreamer-gpu` job never runs (GitHub GPU runner not enabled on the account), DMV3-10 appears untested in the milestone audit. Why: macOS has no NVIDIA GPU and the existing CI is CPU-only. Mitigation (D-01/D-02): add the `dreamer-gpu` job on a GitHub-hosted GPU runner firing on merge-to-main + manual dispatch; if the runner is not enabled, document it as an ops enablement step in the PR — do NOT silently leave it 100%-skipped.

**Core Pattern (manual `embodied.Driver` + `agent.train()` batch loop — D-07):**
`_train_loop` owns the JSON pipe directly. It does NOT call `embodied.run.train` — that entry point manages its own checkpointing + logging cadence, which would double-checkpoint and conflict with the subprocess protocol's `CHECKPOINT`/`METRICS` message types (Pitfall: double-checkpointing). The loop:
1. Construct `embodied.Driver(env)` and warmup the replay buffer with initial episodes.
2. `carry = agent.init_train(batch_size)` (recurrent training state).
3. While `int(step) < total_steps`: pull a batch from `replay.dataset(...)`, call `carry, metrics = agent.train(carry, data)`, increment `step`, `yield {"step": int(step), **metrics}` (the parent's `TRAIN` handler ships this as a `METRICS` message via `_JsonStdout`).
4. Periodically (`int(step) % eval_every == 0`): `cp.save()` for DMV3-08 persistence.
5. On parent `CHECKPOINT` messages: `_run_subprocess_loop`'s handler calls `_save_checkpoint(bundle, path)` → `cp.save()`.

```python
# Inline comments are load-bearing — see Pitfalls 1/3/4/5 in Section 3.
def _train_loop(agent_bundle, total_steps, eval_every):
    import embodied  # child-only (SC#5)
    agent, env = agent_bundle["agent"], agent_bundle["env"]
    replay, cp, step = agent_bundle["replay"], agent_bundle["checkpoint"], agent_bundle["step"]
    driver = embodied.Driver(env)                                 # VERIFY ctor
    # Warmup: seed replay before training (dreamerv3 needs transitions to sample from).
    driver(lambda action, state: replay.add(state, action), steps=warmup_steps)  # VERIFY API
    carry = agent.init_train(batch_size)                         # VERIFY signature
    while int(step) < total_steps:
        data = next(replay.dataset(batch_size, chunk_length))    # VERIFY iterator API
        carry, metrics = agent.train(carry, data)                # VERIFY; metrics: dict[str, scalar]
        step.increment(batch_steps)                              # VERIFY Counter API
        # This yield rides _JsonStdout → pipe.send → parent METRICS handler (SC#1 unchanged).
        yield {"step": int(step), **{k: float(v) for k, v in metrics.items()}}
        if int(step) % eval_every == 0:
            cp.save()                                            # DMV3-08 persist
```

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

There is no LLM structured-output loop in the training path. Pydantic IS used at the
parent/subprocess boundary: the parent sends a CONFIG message whose dict is validated into
the project's `DreamerConfig` (`src/surg_rl/scene_definition/schema.py`) before being handed
to the child. The "retry on validation failure" analog is: validation raises → the subprocess
emits an `ERROR` message instead of `CONFIG_ACK` → the parent surfaces it. There is no
auto-retry with a softer schema; config errors are user-facing and must fail closed.

```python
# DreamerConfig (already defined in src/surg_rl/scene_definition/schema.py — NOT modified this phase,
# additive per D-06). Shown here so the stub author knows the validated shape the CONFIG dict must match.
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from typing import Literal


class DreamerConfig(BaseModel):
    """Skeletal DreamerV3 configuration — configures the subprocess (obs_type, isolation, etc.)."""
    obs_type: Literal["pixels", "state"] | None = Field(
        default=None, description="Observation modality (per DMV3-04, None = state default)"
    )
    pixel_resolution: tuple[int, int] | None = Field(
        default=None, description="Render resolution for pixel observations (w, h)"
    )
    process_isolation: bool = Field(
        default=True, description="Run DreamerV3 in isolated JAX subprocess (per DMV3-03)"
    )
    memory_fraction: float = Field(
        default=0.4, ge=0.1, le=0.9,
        description="XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION for JAX subprocess",
    )
    dreamer_variant: Literal["dreamerv3"] = Field(default="dreamerv3")
    reconstruction_metric: Literal["mse", "ssim", "psnr"] = Field(default="mse")


# --- How the framework integrates (parent side, BEFORE spawning the child) ---
# Per CLAUDE.md Pydantic v2 rules:
#  - Model(**data) and Model.model_validate(data) BOTH perform full validation (same path).
#  - To SKIP validation use Model.model_construct(**data) — but nested dicts stay plain dicts,
#    so do NOT use model_construct for the config handed to the child (the child reads
#    config["obs_type"], config["memory_fraction"], etc. — plain-dict access is fine, but
#    nested-model access would AttributeError).
#  - In model_validator(mode="after"), use self.model_copy(update={...}), not mutating self.
#  - Enum values in model_dump() stay as Enum objects — convert before json.dumps/YAML.
cfg = DreamerConfig.model_validate(config_dict)          # full validation; raises ValueError on bad input
# Convert to a JSON-serializable plain dict for the CONFIG message (Enum values would break json).
payload = cfg.model_dump(mode="json")
# ... send {"type": "CONFIG", "config": payload} to the child ...

# --- Retry logic (analog) ---
# 1 attempt. On pydantic.ValidationError:
#   - log the full error (cfg.__class__.__name__ + the error repr),
#   - emit a parent-side error (do NOT spawn the child with a half-validated config),
#   - surface to the user — no silent fallback to defaults, no re-prompt.
# This matches the subprocess protocol's ERROR message semantics; there is no LLM to re-query.
```

Framework integration summary for this `framework + system_type`:
- **Construction:** `DreamerConfig.model_validate(config_dict)` at the parent, `model_dump(mode="json")` to serialize for the pipe. The child receives a plain dict and reads keys directly (it does not re-validate — Pydantic is a parent-side dep, and the child must not require it for JAX isolation).
- **Retry:** single-shot, fail-closed. Validation failure is a user config bug, not a model flake — no retry loop, no softer schema. Log the pydantic error repr; surface as ERROR.
- **What to log:** the field that failed + the validator that rejected it (pydantic gives both in `e.errors()`). Do NOT log the full config if it contains secrets (this one does not).

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

This system is NOT async-first — it uses a synchronous `multiprocessing.spawn` child process
with a blocking `Pipe.recv()` loop (`_run_subprocess_loop`). There is no `asyncio` event loop
in the subprocess or the parent. The "one common mistake" in this domain is NOT an
`asyncio.run()`-in-a-loop mistake; it is the **JAX-import-ordering / blocking-pipe** mistake:

- **The common mistake:** calling `import jax` (or importing any module that transitively
  imports jax) BEFORE `os.environ["XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION"]` is set. JAX reads these
  env vars at import time; setting them after import has NO effect (verified — JAX docs +
  discussion #16177). The existing code sets them at `subprocess.py:19-20` inside
  `_subprocess_main` BEFORE `_run_subprocess_loop` does `import jax` at line 60. This ordering
  is load-bearing and must be preserved when the stubs are replaced.
- **Stream vs. await analog:** the subprocess "streams" METRICS to the parent per batch
  (the parent's `train()` generator yields METRICS as they arrive — UX streaming, so the
  researcher sees training progress). The parent "awaits" structured results for EVAL
  (one `EVAL_RESULT` message — structured-output validation, not streamed). Keep this
  split: per-batch METRICS are streamed for observability; EVAL_RESULT is a single awaited
  structured payload.
- **Do NOT introduce `asyncio`** into the subprocess to "make it faster" — JAX's
  `agent.train()` is already async internally (via `ThreadPoolExecutor` for saves); adding
  an event loop on top races with the blocking `Pipe.recv()` and the `_JsonStdout` writer.
  The protocol is deliberately synchronous-blocking.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

N/A for RL training — there is no LLM context window. No RAG chunking, no conversation
summarisation, no agent compaction. The policy's "memory" is the RSSM recurrent state
(`carry`), which is fixed-shape and bounded — it does not grow with episode length.

RL analogs that ARE bounded (manage these, but do not call them "context management"):
- **Replay buffer capacity:** `embodied.replay.Replay(length=...)` — FIFO eviction; set
  explicitly from config. Unbounded replay = OOM on long runs.
- **Batch/chunk length:** `batch_length` for the RSSM sequence — longer = more memory per
  train step; tune via config, do not leave unbounded.
- **Observation resolution:** `pixel_resolution` (default `(64, 64)`) for `obs_type="pixels"`
  — larger = higher GPU memory per batch; bounded by the `DreamerConfig` field.
- **No compaction needed:** unlike an LLM agent whose context grows turn-by-turn, the
  subprocess's state is the agent weights + replay + step counter, all checkpointed and
  resumed verbatim (Section 4 State Management). There is nothing to compact.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

No per-call LLM cost (no hosted model). The budget is **GPU-hours + metered-runner spend**.
- **Per-run cost estimate at expected volume:** CI smoke = `total_steps=1000` on a GitHub
  GPU runner (`ubuntu-latest-4-core-gpu`), ~3-5 min/run (per Phase 30 D-STEPS-01). At
  merge-to-main + manual dispatch frequency (D-02 — NOT per-PR), this is a small number of
  GPU-runner minutes per week. Production training runs (100K+ steps) are the real cost
  center and are out of scope for this phase's CI.
- **Caching strategy:** checkpoint resume is the "cache" — restarting a run with the same
  `{task}_{obs_type}` dir resumes from the last `checkpoint.ckpt` instead of paying for
  re-training from step 0 (D-09). This is the single biggest cost lever; verify resume
  works (DMV3-08 test) so partial runs are never thrown away.
- **Cheaper-model-for-sub-tasks analog:** DreamerV3 has no sub-task model routing, but the
  same principle applies to *compute* routing:
  - **Regression guard + import-leak guard:** run on CPU (no GPU) on every PR — cheap,
    fast, catches `_build_agent returns None` and JAX-leak regressions before they reach
    the GPU runner (D-10).
  - **Structural-only smoke assertions:** finite + non-explosive loss + `checkpoint.ckpt`
    exists — NOT convergence thresholds (`MSE<0.01`). Convergence is a separate, more
    expensive job class (explicitly excluded by DMV3-10).
  - **`obs_type="state"` for resume/CI:** low-dimensional state obs are far cheaper per
    step than pixels; use `state` for the resume test and the CI smoke where convergence
    is not the goal.
- **Latency:** the subprocess pipe adds ~1 round-trip per METRICS message (~100 messages
  per 1000 steps — negligible). The dominant latency is `agent.train()` on GPU; this is
  inherent to the algorithm, not a framework overhead to optimize away in this phase.

## 6. Guardrails

> **Adaptation note (load-bearing):** For an RL training integration there are no
> online user-facing safety guardrails — no end-user sees the subprocess output,
> there is no content to moderate, no user query to refuse. The "online" guardrails
> below are the **regression-guard tests that fail the build** if the stub regresses
> or JAX leaks — they run on every PR (CPU, fast) and block merge. The "offline
> flywheel" is the **merge-to-main GPU smoke run** sampling training stability over
> time. This is an honest adaptation of the guardrail/flywheel distinction to an RL
> training pipeline, not a fabricated content-moderation guardrail.

### Online (Real-Time — Runs Every PR, Blocks Merge)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Stub-regression guard (`test_dreamerv3_regression_guard.py`) | `_build_agent` can `return None` (AST `Return(Constant(None))` or `Name(None)` detected in source) | Block — fail the PR build. The historical regression (`_build_agent returns None` shipped once before) is the failure mode this guard prevents. No "flag and continue" — this fails closed. |
| JAX import-leak guard (`tests/test_dreamer_subprocess.py`) | `jax` or `dreamerv3` in `sys.modules` after `import surg_rl.dreamer` in the parent; OR top-level `import jax`/`import dreamerv3`/`import embodied` in `subprocess.py` (AST-detected outside function bodies) | Block — fail the PR build. JAX leaking into the parent preallocates 75% of GPU memory and breaks the macOS/parent import path. Fails closed. |
| Sentinel-flip anti-regression (string scan on the flipped E2E file) | The flipped E2E file contains `pytest.raises(RuntimeError, match="Agent not configured")` again (someone un-flipped the sentinel) | Block — fail the PR build. The sentinel must stay inverted; un-flipping is a regression. Fails closed. |
| CI firing-model guard (YAML structural check on `ci.yml`) | The `dreamer-gpu` job fires on `pull_request` (per-PR GPU spend burn); OR the job's `total_steps` is not 1000 (convergence-budget drift) | Block — fail the PR build. Metered GPU-runner spend is the stakes; per-PR firing burns budget silently. Fails closed. |
| `skipif` gate (INV-8 macOS skip) | Running GPU-gated tests on a macOS dev box without a GPU/dreamerv3/jax | Skip — `pytestmark = pytest.mark.skipif(...)` union gate. NOT a failure — the skip is the correct behavior on macOS; the GPU runner is where these run for real. |

### Offline (Flywheel — Merge-to-Main GPU Smoke, Feeds Improvement Loop)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Training-loop finiteness (NaN/Inf in METRICS) | Every merge-to-main GPU smoke run (1000 steps). The full METRICS stream is inspected — not sampled. | If any NaN/Inf: the merge is broken — investigate `agent.train()` stability (KL spikes, symlog/symexp, LaProp+AGC). ## 7. Production Monitoring

> **Adaptation note (load-bearing):** This is a research training pipeline, NOT a
> deployed user-facing service. The LLM-tracing platforms in the `ai-evals.md`
> tooling table (Arize Phoenix, Langfuse, LangSmith) do NOT fit an RL training
> subprocess — they trace LLM spans (prompt → model → response), and there are
> none here. Installing one would be cargo-cult. The honest mapping is:
> - **"Tracing"** → the `METRICS` messages emitted over the JSON-stdio pipe
>   (already structured: `{"step": int, "<metric>": float, ...}`) + the dreamerv3
>   logger on stderr + the CI/GitHub Actions run log.
> - **"Alerting"** → CI job failure on merge-to-main (the GPU smoke is the alarm).
> - **"Sampling"** → the merge-to-main cadence (D-02: not per-PR) is the sampling
>   strategy — every merge samples training stability once.
>
> N/A for an LLM tracing platform — monitoring is the CI smoke job + the stderr
> logger + the JSON-stdio METRICS stream, not an LLM tracing platform.

**Tracing Tool:** The project's existing JSON-over-stdio subprocess protocol + the dreamerv3 logger (stderr) + GitHub Actions run telemetry. No external tracing platform introduced (Arize Phoenix / Langfuse / LangSmith are LLM-tracing tools — wrong problem class; see adaptation note).

**Tracing surface (what "monitoring" actually is here):**
- **`METRICS` over the JSON-stdio pipe** — per-batch `{"step": int, "loss": float, "kl": float, ...}` emitted by `_train_loop`'s `yield`, shipped to the parent via `_JsonStdout`, logged by the parent. This IS the training trace. It is already structured JSON; no instrumentation framework needed.
- **dreamerv3 logger on stderr** — the embodied `Logger`/`TerminalOutput` writes human-readable `loss=0.5 kl=0.01 ...` lines to `sys.stderr` (redirected per Pitfall 5 / D-locked decision). Captured in the CI run log for post-hoc inspection.
- **CI run log** — the GitHub Actions `dreamer-gpu` job log is the durable record of each merge-to-main smoke run. Used for the 100%-skipped audit (Section 6 offline flywheel).
- **`checkpoint.ckpt` + `config_resolved.yaml`** — the on-disk reproducibility trail (git SHA, config, step counter) per `models/dreamerv3/{task}_{obs_type}/`. The researcher inspects this when resuming.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Per-batch loss finiteness** — `all(math.isfinite(v) for v in metrics.values())` per batch over the 1000-step smoke. The primary "training didn't blow up" signal.
2. **Loss order-of-magnitude bound** — `max(loss) <= 10 * loss[0]`. The structural-stability signal (NOT convergence — explicitly excluded by DMV3-10).
3. **Checkpoint file existence** — `Path(".../checkpoint.ckpt").exists()` after the run. The "training actually persisted" signal.
4. **Resume step-counter continuity** — `int(step) >= 500` after restart-then-continue. The "checkpoint is loadable and real" signal (DMV3-08).
5. **JSON-pipe decode error count** — `JSONDecodeError` count == 0 across the full stdout stream. The "pipe stayed clean" signal (SC#1/SC#5).
6. **`dreamer-gpu` job liveness** — non-skipped run count in the audit window. The "DMV3-10 is actually being verified" signal (the 100%-skipped audit-failure mode).

**Alert Thresholds:**
- **Any NaN/Inf in any METRICS value** → CI job failure on merge-to-main. Investigate stability (KL spikes, symlog/symexp, LaProp+AGC) before the next merge.
- **Loss explodes beyond 10x initial** → CI job failure. Investigate gradient clipping / learning rate before the next merge.
- **`checkpoint.ckpt` not written** → CI job failure. The run produced no persistent artifact — investigate `cp.save()` cadence and `eval_every` firing.
- **Resume restarts at step 0** → CI job failure on the resume test. Investigate `embodied.Checkpoint` attribute registration.
- **`JSONDecodeError` count > 0** → CI job failure. Investigate embodied logger stdout redirection.
- **`dreamer-gpu` job 100%-skipped in the milestone audit window** → milestone verification fails. Document the GitHub GPU runner enablement blocker (do NOT silently close the milestone with DMV3-10 unverified).
- **No "page" / on-call rotation** — this is a research pipeline, not a deployed service. Alerts are CI failures on merge-to-main, surfaced to the researcher who merged the change. There is no pager.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- **The sampling IS the firing model (D-02):** merge-to-main + manual `workflow_dispatch` + release tags. Every merge samples training stability once; no per-PR sampling (metered GPU-runner spend). This is the smart-sampling decision — weight toward the highest-signal moment (a merge is a human-accepted change) and skip the noisy low-signal moment (a PR is exploratory).
- **Researcher qualitative sampling (periodic, not per-merge):** the RL researcher periodically rolls out `agent.policy` on a checkpoint and eyeballs whether the policy is degenerate vs. learning. This is the analog of "human review of edge cases" from `ai-evals.md` — applied to RL, it is "is the policy actually doing something surgical, or reward-hacking a shaped reward". This is NOT a scored rubric and NOT in CI; it is the researcher's judgment call that feeds the future convergence-validation phase (out of scope for DMV3-10).
- **Signal-metric divergence (the `ai-evals.md` early-warning concept, adapted):** if the structural smoke passes (finite loss, checkpoint exists) but the researcher's qualitative rollout shows a degenerate policy, THAT is the signal-metric divergence — the structural metrics say "training ran" but the human signal says "the policy is bad". This is exactly why DMV3-10 excludes convergence thresholds for this phase: the smoke answers "did training run", NOT "is the policy good". The divergence is expected and is the trigger for a future convergence-validation phase, not a reason to re-introduce `MSE<0.01` into the smoke.
